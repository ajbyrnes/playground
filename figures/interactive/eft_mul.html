A = D(1) + D(2) + …  (split by rows)

B = E(1) + E(2) + …  (split by columns)

A and B are shaded by exponent (darker = larger). Every row of A spans the same number of bits, rotated so each column mixes large and small entries; likewise every column of B. A's slices are bands along its rows, B's along its columns.

The nA × nB products D(r) E(s)

One cell per product, shaded by the fraction of its entries that are nonzero. Each fl(D(r) E(s)) is exact, and all of them add up to AB exactly. Products that are entirely zero (empty cells) are where the paper's sparse storage pays off.

Slice counts vs span

nA depends only on A's row spans and nB only on B's column spans, each growing about one slice per α bits. The work is nA · nB matrix products, so it grows with the product of the two.
